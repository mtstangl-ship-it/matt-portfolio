01 · BriefThe real problem

The brief said vaccination rates. The real problem was trust — and where trust lives.

EY recruited me to lead experience design on Georgia Department of Public Health's COVID-19 vaccination engagement. In the communities we needed to reach most, vaccine uptake had plateaued. Mass marketing wasn't converting. In some audiences, it was amplifying resistance — the institutional voice was the wrong voice.

The design question: where does trust actually live, and how does a public-health program show up inside it without colonizing it?

02 · FrameThree goals, one program

Confidence. Awareness. Access.

Say YES Summer was framed against three goals — in that order. Confidence had to come before awareness. Awareness had to come before access. A person who didn't trust the vaccine wouldn't convert no matter how visible the booth was.

Design principle

Make vaccination available — don't make it the reason people show up. People came for the Bananas game, the Twilight Festival, the Aquarium. The vaccine was on the same block. That reframing was the whole program.

Three partner categories, three different trust currencies:

  • VACCINATIONCORE Georgia · local Health DistrictsOn-site clinical delivery · Pfizer + J&J, choice preserved
  • COMMUNITYLiving Walls · local artists · musiciansMurals, live art, bluegrass — people came for this
  • PUBLIC HEALTHGeorgia DPH · CORE personnel on-siteConversations, not campaigns · facts on request
03 · FieldWhat we learned on-site

Four takeaways a dashboard couldn't have produced.

Every event generated learnings that shaped the next one. By Atlanta, the program was measurably different from Savannah — not because the metrics changed, but because the mechanisms had been understood. These four became the operating principles for the program and every regional expansion afterward.

ON-THE-GROUND TAKEAWAYS · SAY YES SUMMER 2021

Four mechanisms the booth made visible.

01 WHO PEOPLE TRUSTED

Loved ones were the deciders.

Many event attendees were brought to the site by a family member or friend — the loved one had decided for them before they arrived.

DESIGN IMPLICATION Make it easy to bring someone. Never design for the hesitant individual in isolation — they don't decide in isolation.
02 WHY PEOPLE STALLED

Information noise wasn't pro- or anti-. It was paralyzing.

Hesitant attendees cited "a lot of information from both sides" and didn't know what to believe. The pause wasn't conviction. It was overload. They wanted a human, not another ad.

DESIGN IMPLICATION On-site CORE personnel for conversation, not pitch. Questions answered with facts, not persuasion. The booth replaced the algorithm.
03 WHAT AGENCY PRODUCED

Choice was reassurance.

Offering both Pfizer and Johnson & Johnson — and letting the final choice be theirs — converted people who wouldn't have converted with a single option. Optionality read as respect.

DESIGN IMPLICATION Every field event carried multiple manufacturers from then on. "The vaccine" stopped being monolithic; it became a decision you got to make.
04 WHY THE ART MATTERED

Multiple engagement levels let people reconsider.

Writing on the wall. Watching live art being made. Listening to music. These weren't decoration — they gave bystanders time and distance to reconsider without having to commit. Reconsideration led to more shots in arms.

DESIGN IMPLICATION Every event needed at least three things to do besides vaccinate. The vaccine was always available. It was never the first thing asked of you.
04 · ImpactWhat 715 means

715 is small on a dashboard. It's enormous against a hesitancy baseline.

The number that mattered wasn't the one on the page. It was the one that didn't exist yet: what does it cost to convert a vaccine-hesitant person through mass channels? In most credible estimates from that period, the answer was "we couldn't." Say YES Summer produced 715 actual conversions in 40 live hours — at a per-conversion level of trust-building no digital campaign could produce.

The awareness layer (4.57M impressions, 350K site visits) did its job too — but as scaffolding, not conversion. It told people the program existed. The activation layer did the actual work. Two motions. One program. Measured separately.

05 · LessonWhat the field taught the program
Public health works where the public lives. The vaccine was always available. The vaccine was never the point.

Say YES Summer became Georgia DPH's blueprint for subsequent community engagement. The four takeaways above became the operating principles. The partner list grew. The model repeated.

The lesson I carry forward: when conversion stalls, don't louden the megaphone. Ask where trust actually lives, and show up there without trying to own it. Bring the vaccine. Don't bring the agenda.
