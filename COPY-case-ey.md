The brief said vaccination rates. The real problem was trust — and where trust lives.

By August 2021, vaccination rates in Georgia had stalled where they were hardest to move. ## 03 · Approach

**Stage label**
```
03 · APPROACH · CONFIDENCE BEFORE AWARENESS BEFORE ACCESS
```

**Headline**
```
Confidence. Awareness. Access. In that order.
```

**Body**
```
Say YES Summer was framed against three goals, **sequenced rather than stacked**. Confidence had to come before awareness. Awareness had to come before access. A person who didn't trust the vaccine wouldn't convert no matter how visible the booth was.

The structural design call: make the vaccine available — don't make it the reason people show up. People came for the Savannah Bananas home game. They came for the Twilight Criterium festival in Athens. They came for the Georgia Aquarium in Atlanta. The vaccine was on the same block. The vaccine was always available. The vaccine was never the first thing asked of you.

Three partner categories carried different trust currencies:

VACCINATION — CORE Georgia and local Health Districts handled clinical delivery. Pfizer and Johnson & Johnson on offer. Choice preserved.
COMMUNITY — Living Walls, local artists, local musicians. Murals being painted. Live bluegrass. Bystanders had reasons to stay that weren't the vaccine.
PUBLIC HEALTH — Georgia DPH and CORE personnel on-site for conversation, not pitch. Questions answered with facts when asked. The booth replaced the algorithm.

24+ partners across the three categories. 10 events across the three cities. Every event had at least three things to do besides vaccinate.
```

## 05 · Delta

**Stage label**
```
05 · DELTA · WHAT 715 ACTUALLY MEANS
```

**Headline**
```
715 is small on a dashboard. It's enormous against a hesitancy baseline.
```

**Body**
```
The number that mattered wasn't the one on the page. It was the one that didn't exist yet: what does it cost to convert a vaccine-hesitant person through mass channels?

In most credible estimates from that period, the answer was: we couldn't. Mass marketing produced awareness, sometimes resistance, rarely conversion in this audience. Say YES Summer produced 715 actual conversions in 40 live hours — at a per-conversion level of trust-building that no digital campaign could match.

The awareness layer (4.57M social impressions, 350K visits to sayyessummer.com) did its job too — but as scaffolding, not conversion. Two motions, one program design, measured separately.

The CDC named Say YES Summer a national best practice for community engagement. Georgia DPH used the four field-learning takeaways as operating principles for every regional expansion that followed.
```

## 06 · Lesson

**Stage label**
```
06 · LESSON · WHAT THE BOOTH MADE VISIBLE
```

**Headline**
```
Four mechanisms a dashboard couldn't have produced.
```

**Body — four field learnings**
```
01 · WHO PEOPLE TRUSTED · Loved ones were the deciders.
Many event attendees were brought to the site by a family member or friend — the loved one had decided for them before they arrived. Design implication: make it easy to bring someone. Never design for the hesitant individual in isolation. They don't decide in isolation.

02 · WHY PEOPLE STALLED · Information noise wasn't pro- or anti-. It was paralyzing.
Hesitant attendees cited "a lot of information from both sides" and didn't know what to believe. The pause wasn't conviction; it was overload. Design implication: on-site CORE personnel for conversation, not pitch. Questions answered with facts, not persuasion. The booth replaced the algorithm.

03 · WHAT AGENCY PRODUCED · Choice was reassurance.
Offering both Pfizer and Johnson & Johnson — and letting the final choice be theirs — converted people who wouldn't have converted with a single option. Optionality read as respect. Design implication: every field event carried multiple manufacturers from then on. "The vaccine" stopped being monolithic; it became a decision you got to make.

04 · WHY THE ART MATTERED · Multiple engagement levels let people reconsider.
Writing on the wall. Watching live art being made. Listening to music. Live art, music, and murals gave bystanders a reason to stay and the distance to reconsider without having to commit. Reconsideration led to more shots in arms. Design implication: every event needed at least three things to do besides vaccinate.
```

**Closing line**
```
Public health works where the public lives. The vaccine was always available. The vaccine was never the point.
```
