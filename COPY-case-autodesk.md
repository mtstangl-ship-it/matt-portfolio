## 03 · Approach

**Stage label**
```
03 · APPROACH · DESIGN AS DIRECTIONAL CLARITY
```

**Headline**
```
We didn't design a workflow. We designed the directional document.
```

**Body**
```
The work ran as a CSXD design sprint with a four-phase methodology: Understand, Define, Develop, Deliver. The team interviewed 37 GTM employees, prototyped with 34 customers, surveyed 288 employees, validated with 25 manager prototypes. The output was a Future Vision for customer planning — an end-to-end journey, articulated in the customer's language, designed to be deliverable across two service tiers.

Five phases anchored the journey: Identify outcomes (CBIs) → Evaluate solutions → Create a plan → Execute the plan → Assess the value. Each phase had defined jobs-to-be-done, customer-facing experiences, and the backstage capabilities (people, process, platform) required to deliver them.

Two service tiers split the audience by signal: Growth Plus (large accounts, high-touch, ~700-1,000 customers) and Nurture Plus (large accounts, medium-touch, ~400-600 customers). Same journey. Different delivery assembly. The tier wasn't a discount mechanism — it was a way to scale outcome-led delivery without losing the high-touch where it earned its weight.
```

```

**Body**
```
The artifact below is the rebuilt Service Assembly — five phases of customer planning, exploded into frontstage activities and backstage capabilities, with a tier toggle that reveals how Growth Plus and Nurture Plus deliver against the same journey through different mechanisms.

Phase 01 · Identify outcomes — Establish trust. Inform and inspire decision-makers. Align and prioritize the outcomes worth investing in.
Phase 02 · Evaluate solutions — Assess current state. Design and validate new solutions. Create and present the business case.
Phase 03 · Create a plan — Define milestones, metrics, roles, responsibilities. Prioritize and schedule. Source and customize content.

The differentiation lives in the backstage. Customers see five phases. The design lived in what made those five phases run.

## 05 · Delta

**Stage label**
```
05 · DELTA · WHAT THE DESIGN SET IN MOTION
```

**Headline**
```
The design's downstream consequences were larger than the design itself.
```

**Body**
```
The 106% NRR projection required a journey the existing tech stack couldn't deliver. The design made that gap visible. Once visible, it had to be closed.

What followed wasn't part of the design brief. It was the org's response to a directional document it now believed in:

100+ CX and EX outcomes surfaced and prioritized — gathered through the prototype testing and synthesis cycle, then handed off to product, engineering, and operations teams as the working backlog for "make the journey real."

8-figure tech modernization initiated — to support delivery of the journey across Sales, Success, Product, Partners, and the customer-facing platform. The platforms that needed to talk to each other (Gainsight, Salesforce, the planning workspace, the customer health system) hadn't been designed to. The journey design surfaced what coordination required.

5 program areas mobilized — GRO (program management, business operations), CSD (CX design, offering management, success/value practice), ESE (product management, UX), DXC (product, UX). Each took ownership of initiative roadmaps tied to specific motions in the design.

I led the design. I earned the modernization work for my larger org. The design demanded it.
```

## 06 · Lesson

```
06 · LESSON · WHAT THE DESIGN TAUGHT THE ORG
```

**Headline**
```
Three notes from the design rationale.
```

**Body — three rationale notes**
```
01 · TRADEOFF · Differentiated experience vs. scalable delivery.
Two tiers share one journey. Growth Plus runs 1:few, high-touch. Nurture Plus runs 1:many, digital-guided. Same phases — different delivery assembly. The differentiation isn't in what the customer experiences; it's in how the work gets staffed and routed behind the scenes.

02 · SYSTEM · Backstage did the work.
Customers saw five phases. Behind them: enabled people on common methods, shared process across Sales/Success/Partners, telemetry-driven platform across Gainsight and SFDC. The design was the backstage, not the journey label. The journey was the customer's surface; the assembly was the org's commitment.

03 · OUTCOME · The investment was the validation.
The org's response to the design — 100+ outcomes prioritized, 8-figure tech modernization initiated, 5 program areas mobilized — was the proof the directional document worked. Companies don't put eight figures behind designs they don't believe in. The 106% NRR path now has the infrastructure to be delivered against.
```
