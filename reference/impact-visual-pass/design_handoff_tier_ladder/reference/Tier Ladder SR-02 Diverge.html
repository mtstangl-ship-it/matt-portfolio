§ 0 · DIAGNOSIS · WHAT SR-01 GOT WRONG AT THE CONCEPT LEVEL ~200 WORDS

SR-01's three variations shared one unstated assumption: that a single canvas, in a single grammar, could carry sequence, hierarchy, and inheritance at co-equal weight. All three were Gantt-with-chip-cluster hybrids. The Gantt brought the time axis. The chip cluster brought the inventory. Neither could be subordinated because both were sized to compete.

That assumption produced predictable failures. VAR A's T02-active layout exception wasn't an edge case — it was the canvas confessing that four chips don't fit in one month-cell when chips are sized to also carry inheritance markers. VAR B's 280px manifest column wasn't a trade-off — it was the timeline conceding half its width because the chip inventory needed somewhere to live at full size. VAR C's "we'll need to expose the inheritance math at the lane label too" wasn't a polish note — it was the diagram admitting it wasn't carrying the math visually.

When you reach for a layout exception, an external annotation, or a redundant readout, the visualization isn't doing the work. The geometry should be the math. SR-02 fixes this by picking one hero story per variation and demoting the other two to readable subordinates — small, dim, off-stage, or revealed-on-tap. Three relationships, three diagrams, one hero each.

VAR E · HERO & SUBORDINATION

  • Hero. The concentric ring composition. T01 contains T02 contains T03 — visible geometry, not annotation.
  • Demoted: time. "LAUNCHED JUL 2025" lives as one line of the right-edge tier stamp. Not on the diagram.
  • Demoted: inheritance. The lit-set IS the inheritance. No chip duplication. No "↓ INH" callouts. Counting dots = counting services.

HOW IT SHOWS INHERITANCE WITHOUT DUPLICATION

  • Each chip exists in exactly one place: its native tier's ring.
  • Tap T01 → all 15 dots light (T01's 6 + T02's 4 + T03's 5). Tap T02 → 9 light (T02 + T03). Tap T03 → 5 light.
  • The lit area is the math. SR-01's failure mode — repeating the inheritance count as annotation — is gone.

INTERACTION

  • Tap a tier stamp (right-edge) or any dot in its ring → that tier becomes active. Lit set updates.
  • Hover/tap a dot → full service name appears as a fiche leader (shown here for T01).

WHAT STRAINS

  • Outer ring perimeter has limited space for 6 chip-labels at-once. We show them as fiche leaders extending outward — works at this scale; tight on narrow viewports.
  • "Concentric containment" doesn't read as "time-sequenced" — visitors who arrive expecting a roadmap may need a beat to recalibrate. Time stamps on the right help but don't lead.
  • Risk of reading as target / scope rather than inheritance. Mitigation: ring labels say "+6 / +4 / +5" not just service counts.

↘ Comparison & recommendation · SR-02

§ 1 · WHICH HERO MATCHES THE PAGE'S REGISTERReading the variations against the rest of Impact

The Impact page elsewhere treats time as structural — engagement years, hero metric strips, the FIG-stamped timeline patterns we've already shipped. The page's grammar is technical-illustration with date stamps; sequence is the page's natural axis. VAR D fits this register most cleanly. The launch flags read like the rest of the page's date-pinned fiche.

VAR E is the most novel — concentric rings appear nowhere else on Impact. That's a feature (the diagram earns its own visual identity) and a risk (it doesn't share grammar with siblings). It fights the page only if Impact is meant to be tonally uniform top-to-bottom; if "each tab gets its own diagram" is acceptable, the ring is a strong outlier.

VAR F reads as a chart, not a drawing. The big "5 → 9 → 15" headline is closest to the Impact hero-metric strip pattern — that's a tonal match. But the cell-grid below feels like a Marimekko / set-theory diagram, which doesn't appear elsewhere on the page. Mixed register fit.

§ 2 · WHICH HERO IS THE RIGHT ONE FOR THIS STORYWhat the tier ladder is actually about

The tier ladder's most important truth, for a reader evaluating Matt's work at Autodesk, is not "we phased launches across 12 months". Phased launch sequencing is what shipped, but it's not what makes the work credible. What makes the work credible is: we designed a cumulative service inheritance model where higher tiers genuinely contain lower-tier services, and we did it at coherent population scale (950 / 25K+ / 1M+). That's the architecture story. Phased launch is the rollout footnote.

If that read is right, VAR D understates the most important relationship. It treats inheritance as a count in a sidecar — accurate but unweighted. VAR E and VAR F both make inheritance the hero. Between them, VAR E shows inheritance as containment (the structural truth) and VAR F shows it as arithmetic (the proof).

§ 3 · WHAT CHANGES IF THE TIMELINE WAS NEVER THE RIGHT FRAMEPermission used

Reading SR-01 and SR-02 together, I now believe the timeline direction was a strategic choice that became a visual constraint. "Phased launch" is a true and meaningful story but it isn't the load-bearing one for a portfolio reader. The previous chat picked timeline because (a) it gave a strong calendar moment, (b) mobile rotation worked, and (c) it was fiche-native. Those are real, but (a) is a moment, not the story; (b) is fine but mobile-friendliness shouldn't drive desktop concept; (c) is true of any axis-based diagram including rings and bracket-nests.

I'd put the timeline direction down. The cost is losing the JUL / DEC launch moments as scenography. The win is putting the structural story — cumulative inheritance — in the seat of the diagram, which is where the credit lives.

RECOMMEND

Build Variation E · concentric rings, with a small VAR D launch-strip pinned above as the introductory "scene-set".

Why E. It's the only variation where the hero relationship (inheritance) is carried by geometry visitors can read without instructions. Lit-area = service count. Containment = inheritance. Zero chip duplication.

Why a small launch strip above. The phased-launch story is real and we shouldn't lose it entirely. A thin one-line timeline pinned above the ring diagram ("JAN — JUL — DEC · 12 months · three launches") gives the date pegs without compromising the hero. This is intentional subordination, not a fourth dashboard.

What this kills. The Gantt-with-chip metaphor from SR-01. The 280px manifest column. The "inheritance shown three ways" problem.

Mobile. Rings scale by SVG viewBox. Stamps stack. No layout exception. Already validated in the mobile sketch.

Open risk. Concentric rings don't appear elsewhere on Impact. If that becomes a visual coherence problem during build, we fall back to VAR F (cells + brackets) — the headline matches Impact's hero-metric register, and the inheritance story still lands.

§ 4 · OPEN QUESTIONS FOR MATTResolve before build

  • Is the cumulative inheritance story actually the load-bearing one? If the portfolio read is "phased launch was the achievement", the recommendation flips back toward VAR D.
  • Tolerance for tonal outliers on Impact. Concentric rings appear nowhere else. Acceptable as "each tab gets its diagram", or does Impact need grammar uniformity?
  • How much of the launch story can we sacrifice? A pinned strip above the rings carries the date pegs but loses the "duration in market" read VAR D had.
  • The diagnosis pass-criteria above — do they match how you'd evaluate? If not, we're using the wrong rubric.
  • Should we test VAR E and VAR F as a paired flip (rings as the "T01 active" default, brackets as the "compare tiers" alt-view)? Or commit to one geometry?
  • Does Autodesk's actual product structure match the strict 5 / 9 / 15 cumulative model, or are there services in T01 that are NOT in T02 + T03's union? If there are deviations, both E and F need an exception path — go back to the diagnosis.
