Tap a pin and the map zooms from Georgia into the city.

SR-02's balloon, corner-manifest and bottom-rail all fought the same tension — card vs. geography on one canvas. This round resolves it structurally: the geography changes scale to make room for the card. Three variations, each with its default + ATL-active state, mobile, and notes.

SKETCH

The locked direction: tap a pin → the map zooms from state-level Georgia into a stylized representation of that city's local geography. The card lives in the viewport space the zoom frees up. The map never shrinks into a column — its scale changes, and the narrative changes with it.

Three relationships: (1) default scope — Georgia + three pins, the spread is the hero; (2) active specificity — stylized local topology that feels like Atlanta / Savannah without literal accuracy; (3) return — get back to Georgia with a sense of having traveled and come back.

Georgia is the preserved production SVG (placeholder box). City topology is stylized placeholder — grids, coastline, arterials — labelled [CITY-GRID PLACEHOLDER · STYLIZED TOPOLOGY · PROD SVG TBD]. Real city art is sourced separately at Cursor time.

VAR 1 · ZOOM MECHANIC

MOTIONCamera-pan zoom — Georgia scales up centred on the tapped pin; pin location becomes viewport center, then the right ~44% wipes to plate as the card slides in.
TIMING~420ms ease-in-out · single-fire. No loop.
CAMERAFollows the pin — ATL zoom lands NW-biased, SAV zoom lands SE-biased. Each city lands differently.

CARD PLACEMENT

  • Beside, right. Consistent across all three cities. Card never overlaps the city topology.
  • City keeps ~56% (left), card fills the freed ~44% (right). The split itself is the "space the zoom created".

RETURN

  • Persistent "← GEORGIA" top-left of the city view. Tap → camera zooms back out, split collapses, Georgia re-centers.
  • Esc also returns. Scroll does not auto-return (avoids accidental loss of place).

OTHER CITIES

  • Stacked badges top-right (ATH · SAV). Tap a badge → lateral zoom straight to that city (no Georgia round-trip).
  • Breadcrumb "1 / 3 · ATLANTA" in the split-meta strip keeps sequence legible.

SUMMARY STATE

  • Lives in Georgia (default) state — a 4th badge "PROGRAM · 4.57M" bottom-center. Tapping it shows all three pins lit + the total card, no zoom.

VAR 1 · MOBILE LOGIC

No room for a side-by-side split at 380px. The zoom replaces the Georgia map entirely — only the city is visible — and the card docks directly below it. Vertical stack, not split.

Gesture. Tap a pin to zoom (no pinch — taps are unambiguous). The zoom is the same camera-pan but ends full-bleed rather than split.

Return is a persistent top bar ("← GEORGIA" + breadcrumb), always visible above the city so the visitor is never stranded.

Other cities aren't badges here (no room) — the visitor returns to Georgia and taps the next pin, or swipes the card left/right to advance city-to-city.

VAR 2 · MOBILE LOGIC

Draw-in works beautifully on mobile — the stroke-by-stroke plot is a small-screen-friendly moment that doesn't depend on viewport width. City fills the frame; card overlays the bottom third as a docked callout.

Gesture. Tap to zoom/draw-in. The un-draw reversal on "↺ OUT" is identical to desktop, just full-bleed.

Card overlay vs. below: overlay chosen for mobile too — keeps the city visible as context. The card sits low so the upper topology + breadcrumb stay readable.

Return stamp shrinks to "↺ OUT" top-right; always reachable by thumb.

VAR 3 · ZOOM MECHANIC

MOTIONHard cut + crossfade. Tab selection crossfades the band content (Georgia → city) over ~180ms. Fast, no camera travel.
TIMING~180ms crossfade · single-fire. The quickest of the three — feels like switching a sheet, not flying.
CAMERANone. No zoom illusion — the band simply becomes the city. Most "UI", least "cartographic".

CARD PLACEMENT

  • Below the city band, two-column (copy ‖ stats). Consistent for all cities.

RETURN

  • The "GA" tab is the return. Always present, leftmost. Tap → crossfade back to the Georgia map. Return is never more than one tap away.

VAR 3 · MOBILE LOGIC

The tab strip translates to mobile almost unchanged — 5 narrow tabs across the top. This is the variation that survives 380px most gracefully because it never relied on a zoom illusion or a split.

Stack: tabs → city letterbox → card, all vertical. No layout exception from desktop; just narrower.

Gesture. Tap a tab. Optionally swipe the city band left/right to advance tabs. No pinch.

Return + summary are both tabs (GA, TOT) — same one-tap reach as desktop. Strongest mobile story of the three.

↘ Written Analysis · SR-03

§ 1 · COMPOSITION READWhich lands hardest, where each strains

VAR 2 (draw-in overlay) lands hardest as fiche. The schematic-drawing-itself zoom is the only one of the three that's native to this page's visual grammar — it's a technical illustration plotting itself, not a camera move. The strain is the overlay: the card covers ~30% of the city, the same occlusion problem SR-02's balloon had, only partly excused by the city being stylized.

VAR 1 (split-zoom) lands hardest as spatial narrative. The camera-pan into the pin genuinely sells "we traveled into Atlanta", and the split means the card never fights the map. Its strain is execution: per-city camera framing (ATL lands NW, SAV lands SE) is real logic to build and tune, and the split layout is the most fragile to port.

VAR 3 (tab-stack) lands hardest as a usable interface. Everything is one tap; nothing is ever hidden; it survives mobile untouched. But it barely honors the locked direction — there's no zoom illusion, the band just becomes the city. It's the safe choice that quietly abandons the concept's romance.

§ 2 · ZOOM MECHANICS PER VARIATIONWhat each animation actually looks like

VAR 1 · camera-pan (~420ms). Georgia scales up centered on the tapped pin until the pin is viewport-center at ~6× zoom; the right 44% then wipes to plate and the card slides in from the right edge. Feels like a map app flying in — the most "UI animation" of the spatial options, but grounded by landing on real stylized topology.

VAR 2 · draw-in (~600ms + 150ms). Georgia fades to ~8% opacity while the city grid strokes in via dash-offset (minor streets first, arterials last), then the card fades up. This is the most "fiche" — it looks like a plotter drawing a sheet. This is the one that matches visual-grammar.md's "schematic redrawing itself" principle.

VAR 3 · cut + crossfade (~180ms). No travel. The band's contents crossfade from Georgia to the city. Reads as switching a drawing sheet on a light table — fast and legible, but there's no zoom and therefore no sense of scale change. Most "UI", least "cartographic".

§ 3 · CARD PLACEMENT COMPARISONBeside · overlaid · below

BESIDE (VAR 1)

Never occludes the city. Needs the most horizontal room — only works because the split is the freed space. Breaks at mobile (falls back to below). Best desktop read.

OVERLAID (VAR 2)

Covers ~30% of the city. Excused by stylized topology (no precise geography lost). Works identically desktop + mobile. Most compact, slight occlusion cost.

BELOW (VAR 3)

Zero occlusion, infinite card room. Costs vertical space — city compresses to a letterbox band. Most consistent across breakpoints, least "immersive".

§ 4 · RISKS PER VARIATIONExecution & Cursor-port

VAR 1. Per-city camera framing must be authored (each pin's zoom target + final crop). The split transition (map scaling while a panel wipes in) is the hardest single animation to get smooth, and iOS Safari can stutter on simultaneous transform + layout change. Most dependent on the production city SVGs landing well — a camera that flies into a weak SVG exposes it.

VAR 2. Draw-in depends entirely on the city SVGs being strokeable (clean paths with usable lengths for dash animation). If the prod SVGs are filled shapes rather than strokes, the signature mechanic collapses to a fade. Confirm SVG construction before committing. Otherwise lowest-risk logic (fixed camera).

VAR 3. Lowest risk overall — crossfading tab content is routine, no camera math, no per-city logic. The risk is conceptual, not technical: it may read as "we didn't really build the zoom".

§ 5 · RETURN-INTERACTION COMPARISONWhich return feels most natural

VAR 2's un-draw reversal feels most natural to the concept — the city un-plots and Georgia fades back, preserving the "traveled and returned" loop the brief asks for. VAR 1's "← GEORGIA" + zoom-out is close and more conventional (it's a literal zoom-out). VAR 3's "GA" tab is the most reliable and least romantic — you don't return, you just switch back. For "sense of having traveled", VAR 2 > VAR 1 > VAR 3. For "never get stranded", VAR 3 > VAR 1 > VAR 2.

§ 6 · END-OF-PROGRAM SUMMARY STATEWhere the 4.57M total lives

VAR 1 parks it in Georgia (default) state as a "PROGRAM · 4.57M" badge — you must zoom out to see the total, which reinforces "the whole is seen at state level". VAR 2 gives it its own 4th state that draws in after 3/3 cities are inspected — the most ceremonial, rewards completion. VAR 3 makes it a peer tab (PROG/TOT) — always reachable, never ceremonial. Cinematic-to-quiet runs VAR 2 → VAR 1 → VAR 3, same axis as everything else here.

§ 7 · OPEN QUESTIONS FOR MATTResolve before committing

  • Are the production city SVGs strokes or fills? This single fact decides whether VAR 2's draw-in is even possible. If fills, VAR 2 degrades to a fade and loses its whole reason for being.
  • Is "sense of travel" worth the execution cost? VAR 1 and VAR 2 buy romance with real animation complexity. VAR 3 abandons it for reliability. Which matters more for this audience?
  • Does the zoom concept actually beat SR-02's bottom-rail? VAR 3 is honest enough to admit it's close to a tab UI. If we land on VAR 3, we should ask whether the zoom premise earned its round.
  • How literal should city stylization be? Grid for ATL, coastline for SAV, arterials for ATH — enough to feel like the place, or does that invite "this doesn't look like Athens" nitpicks? A uniform abstract treatment is safer but loses specificity.
  • One mechanic for all three cities, or per-city? Mixing (e.g. draw-in for coastal SAV, split for metro ATL) would fragment the register — recommend committing to one.
  • Mobile parity. VAR 3 is the only one that's truly identical desktop↔mobile. If mobile is a primary surface, that's a strong thumb on the scale toward VAR 3 — is it?
